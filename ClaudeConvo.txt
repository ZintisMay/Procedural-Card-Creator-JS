The primary resource: The article "Unique Cards Explained" on altered.gg is the best deep-dive available. It's written by Jacques Ernoult, the game designer who built the system.

How the generation works — two-tool pipeline:
A first tool called "The Generator" spits out millions upon millions of cards semi-randomly derived from each character's base characteristics (statistics, costs, and abilities) with few boundaries. The goal is to be as broad as possible to get as many candidates before narrowing them down. Altered TCG
A second tool, "The Filter," is an evaluation algorithm that rates the generated cards. It mostly filters out "bad" designs rather than selecting "good" ones, and is set with many parameters including: Rational Game Design (assigning a weight to stats and abilities out of a total budget per card), faction-specific traits (e.g. certain stats are more likely for certain factions), prevention of broken ability interactions, and blocking combinations that are legal but too powerful. Altered TCG
Then human review:
For each family of cards, a team of game designers and professional TCG players manually rate hundreds of cards that came out of The Filter. When cards are considered bad designs, they analyze why and update The Filter to prevent them from being generated again — with the difficulty being that new Filter rules can also prevent good cards from being generated, requiring lots of testing. Altered TCG
Tracking uniqueness:
Every card comes with a unique QR code, allowing players to register their cards on the Altered digital app. In the case where a QR code doesn't work, players provide a picture of the QR code so Equinox can access the card's history and check who claimed it and when. So the tracking is handled via a centralized database tied to each card's QR code at the point of printing. GamelandiaAltered TCG
